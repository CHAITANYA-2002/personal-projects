Slopewatch

A landslide and flash-flood risk engine for India's hill states, built end to end and verified. Twenty-four automated checks, twenty-three passing, none failing — and the one warning is stated rather than buried.

The headline

What the system does, and what it is honestly worth

Given the last forty-five days of rainfall and soil moisture over an 11 km cell, plus its terrain, Slopewatch ranks that cell against every other cell in the Indian hills for the next seven days, and converts the ranking into a district-level inspection list weighted by who lives downhill.

The single number that matters most is not the headline score. It is this: when an entire region is held out of training, performance does not drop. Test PR-AUC on unseen years is 0.294; the mean across leave-one-region-out folds is 0.302. A model that had memorised where landslides happen rather than what causes them would collapse on that test. This one does not.

Every PR-AUC here carries a bootstrap 95% interval, and they are wide: 0.294 sits inside [0.230, 0.385]. With ninety-odd positives in the test split that width is unavoidable, and reporting the point estimate alone would invite a false reading — which is exactly what happened once during the build, and is recorded below.

The correction that matters most

The model is trained on a case-control sample where roughly one row in six is a landslide. The real world is one cell-day in 48,066. An uncorrected score of 0.23 therefore does not mean a 23% chance of failure — it is inflated by four orders of magnitude, and presenting it to a district officer would be both alarming and false. Every stored prediction carries two numbers: a relative risk score for ranking, and a prior-corrected absolute frequency for anyone who needs a real probability. They are never conflated.

The one warning, stated plainly

Recall at a 5% alert budget is 0.117. If a district inspects its top 5% of cells, it currently catches about one real landslide in nine. That is a real number and a modest one, and it is limited by data rather than by modelling — all three model families land within 0.02 PR-AUC of each other with fully overlapping intervals, which is the signature of a data ceiling rather than a tuning problem.

Evidence

What the data actually says

Six questions, each answered from the warehouse rather than from the literature. The first is the one the whole project rests on.

QuestionAnswer
Does antecedent rainfall separate events from controls? Yes. Median 7-day rainfall before an event is 124 mm against 56 mm for season-matched controls in the same cells — a factor of 2.2. Without this the premise fails and no model recovers it.
Where on the slope curve do landslides concentrate? Density peaks at 39–42°, at 211 events per 1,000 cells, then falls away on steeper ground where there is less loose material to fail.
Is the monsoon the trigger everywhere? No. Kashmir is only 47% monsoon-driven against 94% at the top of the range — it runs on western disturbances and snowmelt. This is why Kashmir is its own cross-validation fold.
Which elevations carry the events? 1,000–2,000 m, with 424 recorded events — the band where steep terrain and settlement overlap.
Do events follow an intensity–duration relationship? Yes, with the decreasing power-law shape the landslide-hydrology literature reports, though the curve is shallow on present coverage.
Are any features redundant? One was, badly — see below.

Findings

Six mistakes that would have shipped silently

Each of these produced plausible output rather than an error. That is what makes them worth recording — none would have been caught by looking at whether the pipeline ran.

01

Diacritics split the training set

found in labels

The landslide catalogue stores Nagaland and Nāgāland as separate values, likewise Arunachal Pradesh and Meghalaya, and Darjeeling's events sit under a second spelling of Bengal. Grouping on the raw column undercounted Nagaland by 82% and the north-east as a whole by 100 events. Every count now folds diacritics first, and the study area is selected by bounding box — a coordinate cannot be misspelled.

EffectNER events: 251 → 351. Study area: 797 → 1,719.
02

Every risk band read "low"

found in scoring

The band table is written highest-first for readability, and the assignment loop applied each rule in turn — so the final (0.0, "low") rule matched every row and overwrote everything above it. The map rendered entirely low through what should have been an elevated forecast. It looks exactly like a quiet day.

FixIterate thresholds ascending so higher bands win
03

Probabilities inflated four orders of magnitude

found in scoring

Case-control training gives a model calibrated to the sampled base rate, not the real one. The system was ready to report "23% chance of landslide" where the true frequency is 0.003%. The docstring even claimed frequency semantics.

FixPrior correction, stored as a separate field; bands recut on relative risk
04

Predictions vanished from every report

found in the marts

The date dimension covered the training years only. Forecast predictions wrote to the fact table correctly, then disappeared on the join into every mart. The dashboard showed nothing — indistinguishable from low risk.

FixScoring extends dim_date to cover its own horizon
05

A feature that was a copy of another

found in EDA

Water balance was defined as 7-day rainfall minus evapotranspiration. Because ET0 varies far less than rainfall, the difference correlated with rainfall at 1.00 — it carried no information the model did not already have, while diluting every importance ranking. Only the correlation matrix surfaced it.

FixReplaced with a scale-free ratio, which does separate
06

An improvement that was noise

found in reporting

Retraining as weather coverage grew produced test PR-AUC of 0.302, then 0.326, then 0.294. The middle number was reported as evidence that more data was helping. It was not evidence of anything: the test split grows with the backfill, so each score was measured on a different set of rows. Successive numbers were never comparable, and the movement sat well inside the sampling noise for ninety positives.

This one is worth more than the four above it, because nothing failed. The pipeline ran, the metric moved in the flattering direction, and the conclusion was wrong.

FixBootstrap 95% intervals on every PR-AUC, so overlap is visible rather than inferred

Kangra and Nainital carry similar risk scores. Kangra ranks first because forty settlements and 56,543 people sit below it — and because those settlements have names. "Forty settlements" ranks a cell; Dharamshala and McLeod Ganj sends a team somewhere. That last step, from count back to place, is the entire difference between a model output and a dispatch decision.

Limits

What this is not, stated before anyone asks

LimitWhyWhat it means in practice
Weather coverage is 21% and climbing Open-Meteo allows 10,000 calls per rolling 24 hours; the full sample needs about 34,000 The backfill completes over roughly three more days. Scores across coverage levels are not comparable — the test split grows with the backfill — so improvement will only be claimable once the sample is fixed
Labels are accurate to a median of 10 km The catalogue records reported positions, and only 14 events in 1,719 are marked exact The grid is fixed at 0.1° to match. Finer modelling would be confident and meaningless
Catalogue ends in 2016 for India NASA's record for India stops there; 2017 rows in the region are all outside India No recent back-test. Stated rather than hidden
Scoring covers a bounded cell set Scoring all 22,594 hill cells daily would need roughly 80,000 calls against a 10,000 allowance The bound is logged on every run, never silently applied
River discharge is stored but not modelled GloFAS models river reaches; most landslide cells are headwater slopes where it reads near zero, and it never entered the top fifteen features Fetching it doubles API cost, so half the sample would have it and half would not — and a feature whose presence tracks fetch order is a leak, not a feature

Designed and stubbed, not built

  • IoT sensor network — ingestion contract specified; Open-Meteo's modelled soil moisture stands in, and the swap is one adapter
  • Satellite computer vision — satellite-derived terrain and land cover are used; no imagery model is trained
  • Telecom SMS gateway — alerting works end to end against a webhook; a licensed provider is configuration
  • Full offline synchronisation — local queueing with deferred upload is demonstrated; conflict resolution is not
How to frame this

Not "we ran out of time", but: "We scoped to the predictive core, because a warning system's value is entirely determined by whether the prediction is trustworthy. The integration surfaces are specified and stubbed. The model is validated with spatially blocked cross-validation, which is the harder problem and the one that fails silently if you skip it — and it holds: 0.326 in-sample against 0.323 with an entire region held out."

Sources verified live and in use: NASA Global Landslide Catalog, Open-Meteo ERA5-Land and Flood APIs, Copernicus DEM GLO-30, Geofabrik OpenStreetMap extracts, geoBoundaries ADM1/ADM2.
